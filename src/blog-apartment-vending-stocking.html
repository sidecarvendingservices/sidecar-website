What To Stock In Apartment Community Vending Machines

The best-performing machine in a clubhouse looks almost nothing like the best-performing machine in a gym or office — here's what we've learned stocking for residents specifically.

VR
Vala Co-founder, Sidecar Vending Services · July 25, 2026

Apartment communities are one of our favorite accounts to stock, because the machine isn't competing with a vending machine down the hall — it's competing with a 15-minute drive to the nearest gas station or grocery store, usually at an hour when nobody wants to make that drive. That changes what belongs in the machine. Here's the mix we start with, and how we adjust it once real sales data comes in.

Household basics come first, not snacks

The single highest-performing category in an apartment clubhouse is the stuff residents run out of at 9pm on a Tuesday: laundry detergent pods, dryer sheets, dish soap, paper towels, toilet paper, trash bags, and all-purpose cleaning wipes. The leasing office is closed, the front desk (if there is one) can't sell anything, and the nearest store is a real trip. A machine that solves that problem gets used constantly — and it's the reason we lead with household basics on every apartment install, ahead of chips and soda.

Move-in and move-out spikes

Turnover creates predictable demand spikes worth stocking around: box cutters, packing tape, trash bags (again, in bulk), disinfecting wipes, and phone chargers all move faster during heavy move-in/move-out weeks. If your property manager can give us a rough sense of your lease turnover calendar, we'll adjust restocking ahead of it instead of reacting after the shelf is already empty.

Pet supplies, if the community is pet-friendly

Most of the communities we work with allow pets, and waste bags are one of the most consistent repeat-purchase items we stock — residents buy them again and again rather than as a one-off. Small bags of treats and a basic pet stain/odor wipe round this category out well if your clubhouse or dog run gets regular foot traffic.

Personal care and OTC essentials

Pain relievers, allergy medication, bandages, and feminine hygiene products are things people forget about until they need them immediately — and at 10pm on a Sunday, a vending machine down the hall beats every other option available to them. Phone chargers and cables round out this category; they're inexpensive to stock and sell reliably in any residential setting.

Amenity-specific stock: pool and fitness room

If your community has a pool, sunscreen and flip-flops sell well in-season and cost almost nothing to keep on hand during the summer months. If there's a fitness room, protein bars, electrolyte drinks, and gym towels do the same job there that they'd do in a standalone gym — see our gym vending page for more on that mix. We'll tailor stock to whichever amenities your property actually has rather than assuming a one-size-fits-all clubhouse layout.

Snacks and drinks still matter — they're just not the whole story

None of this replaces the standard mix of drinks, chips, and grab-and-go snacks — those still move steadily, especially right after the mail room or gym. The difference in an apartment account is that they share shelf space with the household and personal-care items above, instead of dominating the whole machine the way they might in an office break room.

How we actually decide your mix

We start every apartment install with the categories above as a baseline, then let the machine's real-time sales data do the rest of the work. If detergent pods are flying off the shelf and the fitness-room protein bars are sitting untouched, we shift the mix accordingly on the next restock — no guesswork, no waiting for a resident to complain to the leasing office.
